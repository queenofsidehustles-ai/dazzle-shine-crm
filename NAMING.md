# Naming

Written 2026-08-26. Options for the SaaS product, per `PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` §4.

**I cannot check domain or trademark availability from here.** Everything below
is a candidate, not a clearance. §4 at the bottom is the vetting process — run it
before you fall in love with one.

---

## The constraints, restated

1. **No cleaning words.** No clean, maid, tidy, sparkle, shine, broom. You're
   expanding to landscaping, detailing, pressure washing and window cleaning.
   ZenMaid locked itself into one vertical forever; that's a cautionary tale, not
   a template.
2. **Say it on a phone call without spelling it.** Your customers are on job
   sites, not reading your business card.
3. **`.com` or don't bother.** Avoid `.io` and `.ai` — this market types `.com`
   by reflex and will land on a stranger's site.
4. **Point at the crew.** Hiring them, scheduling them, paying them, holding them
   to a standard is what you do that nobody else does. The name should carry it.

---

## 1. Crew-forward — the strongest direction

The word survives every vertical you plan to enter. A landscaping crew, a
detailing crew, a cleaning crew.

| Name | Read | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rollcall** | Who's working today. | Ops-native and instantly understood by someone who has never seen the product. Warm, slightly military-precise, memorable. **Strong contender.** |
| **Crewline** | The crew + the day's lineup. | Descriptive, professional, sounds like an established company. Safe in the best sense. |
| **Roster** | Your people and your schedule in one word. | The single best word on this page for what you do. Bare `roster.com` will be unobtainable — needs a modifier (`RosterHQ`, `Rosterly`, `GetRoster`), which weakens it. |
| **CrewKit** | A kit: hiring, pay, SOPs, scheduling. | Friendliest option. Best fit for an audience of first-time owners, which is yours. Slightly less premium at $199. |
| **Lineup** | Today's lineup of jobs and people. | Clean and short. Common word, so trademark and domain will both be a fight. |
| **Crewhaus** | Crew + house. | More premium feel, supports higher pricing. The "haus" spelling costs you a beat on the phone. |
| **OnCrew** | Being on the crew. | Short, app-like, easy to say. Slightly generic. |

## 2. Operations-forward

Names about the work getting done rather than the people doing it.

| Name | Read | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Runsheet** | The trade word for the day's job list. | Most distinctive name here. Ops-native, zero vertical lock-in, unlikely to be taken. Costs one sentence of explanation to anyone outside the trades. |
| **Dayboard** | The board with today's work on it. | Evocative, probably available, slightly abstract. |
| **Daysheet** | Same idea, more literal. | Plainer sibling of Runsheet. |
| **Jobline** | The line of jobs. | Clear but forgettable, and "job" bumps into Jobber. |

## 3. Warm / owner-facing

These lean into your audience — people building their first real business — over
the operational buyer.

| Name | Read | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Toolbelt** | What a tradesperson wears. | Excellent cross-vertical fit and genuinely likeable. `.com` is very likely taken; check early. |
| **Backbone** | The backbone of your service business. | Strong tagline built in. Slightly heavy. |
| **Huddle** | Getting the team together. | Warm, but heavily used in SaaS already. |

## 4. Abstract / brandable

**Kova · Rove · Marlow · Sable · Anchor.** Cheapest to trademark and easiest to
own outright — but you pay for the meaning in marketing spend for years, and you
don't have that budget yet. Not where I'd go.

---

## My shortlist, in order

**1. Rollcall** — *"Rollcall. Know who's working, what they're doing, and what
they're owed."* It says crew, it says daily operations, it works for every
vertical on your roadmap, and a cleaning-company owner gets it in one second with
no explanation. Best balance of memorable and self-explanatory.

**2. Crewline** — the safe professional choice. Nobody will ever be confused by
it, and it will never be the most interesting name in a comparison table.

**3. Runsheet** — the connoisseur's pick. Most distinctive, best story, requires
you to explain it once per conversation forever. Worth it if you like it.

**4. CrewKit** — pick this if you decide your primary buyer is the first-time
owner coming out of your audience rather than the established 8-cleaner company.

## Vetting — do this before committing

Run all five on your top two. It's an afternoon.

1. **USPTO search** — `tmsearch.uspto.gov`, free. Check **class 42** (SaaS) and
   **class 35** (business management services). A live mark in either kills it.
   This is the one that can force a rename after launch, so do it first.
2. **Domain.** Check the `.com`. If it's parked, get a price — a good `.com` at
   $2–5k is a real asset and cheaper than a rename in year two.
3. **Google it** with "software," "cleaning," and "field service."
4. **Handles** — Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube. Your audience lives there
   and consistency matters more for you than for most B2B founders.
5. **The phone test.** Say it out loud, in a sentence, to someone who hasn't seen
   this document: *"We run our cleaning business on Rollcall."* If they ask you to
   spell it, cross it off.

---

## Then lock it before the build

The name goes into the subdomain scheme (`acme.rollcall.com`), the sending
domain, the Stripe product catalogue, the app-store listing and every SOP and
template you seed a new tenant with. Renaming after launch means migrating all of
it plus every customer's bookmarks.

**Decide the name before Phase 1 of `SAAS_ROADMAP.md` starts.** It's the cheapest
decision on the whole roadmap right now and one of the most expensive in
six months.
